When to Buy the Nasdaq-100
Timing guidance for beginners

The Nasdaq-100 holds 100 of the largest non-financial companies on the Nasdaq exchange — it's more concentrated in tech than the S&P 500, which means bigger swings in both directions.

💡 Most beginners do best investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule (like monthly) instead of waiting for the "right" time. This is called dollar-cost averaging — and it matters even more here since the Nasdaq-100 moves around more than broader funds.
Nasdaq-100 — last 12 months (trend shape is illustrative; today's level is real, checked July 30, 2026)

The simple approach most beginners use

  • Invest the same amount every month, no matter what the price is doing that day
  • Since this index swings more than the S&P 500, spreading purchases out matters even more here
  • Removes the stress and guesswork of trying to find the "perfect" moment

Signs some investors watch for

  • The index has recently dropped a lot on tech-sector news — some long-term investors see that as a chance to buy more for the same money
  • You already understand you're taking on more tech-sector concentration than a broader fund like the S&P 500
  • You have extra savings you're confident you won't need for at least 5+ years

Things to know

  • Heavily weighted toward technology — if tech has a bad stretch, this index feels it more than the S&P 500 does
  • Nobody — not even professionals — can consistently predict the "perfect" time to buy
  • Consistency over time tends to matter more than perfect timing
